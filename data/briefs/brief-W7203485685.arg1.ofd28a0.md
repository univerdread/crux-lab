## Challenged premise
**W7203485685.c008**: A third-personal proposition identifying God as actualising a world does not itself represent that actualisation under the first-personal aspect required for source attribution.

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · openai:gpt-5.6-sol*

God’s cognition uses only a rigid identifier, “G,” for the unique being whose intentions directly control which world becomes actual. G considers W0 and W1, judges W1 better, forms the intention expressed as “G will actualise W1,” and W1 consequently becomes actual. G then knows the proposition “G actualised W1 rather than W0 because G intended W1.” The identifier is third-personal: it carries no first-personal mode of presentation. Yet the proposition identifies the source perfectly. It singles out the unique controller, connects that being’s intention to the resulting world, and excludes every alternative source. Nothing further supplied by “I actualised W1” would improve the attribution of the actualisation to G.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: The G-proposition identifies the controller and connects G's intention to W1, but robust responsibility requires representing the outcome as the agent's own doing, not merely as a causally explained event involving that agent. A rigid designator fixes reference without fixing the agent's stance toward the act. — *The objector's example already represents W1 as issuing from G's intention and control. Defender A conceded that the argument did not independently show why the further agent-relative stance matters for responsibility.*
- **Defender B**: The argument should distinguish third-personal source-identifying content from practically self-ascriptive cognition that represents actualisation as issuing from that very agent's agency. The relevant condition concerns intentional agency rather than the grammatical form of a proposition. — *This reformulation preserves a possible route to the conclusion, but it revises rather than establishes c008. It leaves open whether the G-thought can itself have the required practical role despite its third-personal form.*

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the argument from divine contrastive responsibility to irreducible de se content depends on a substantive agency condition, not on the merely third-personal grammar or referential precision of divine cognition. It would develop the distinction between source-identifying knowledge and practically self-ascriptive representation, anticipate the rigid-identifier case in which G's intention selects and produces W1, and show either that this case lacks the agency-relative aspect required for robust responsibility or that no such additional aspect is needed. To succeed, the paper would need to state and defend a responsibility-relevant criterion that distinguishes treating an intentional act as an object of knowledge from representing it as one's own doing.
