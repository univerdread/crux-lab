## Challenged premise
**W7203485685.c008**: A third-personal proposition identifying God as actualising a world does not itself represent that actualisation under the first-personal aspect required for source attribution.

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · openai:gpt-5.6-sol*

God’s cognition uses only a rigid identifier, “G,” for the unique being whose intentions directly control which world becomes actual. G considers W0 and W1, judges W1 better, forms the intention expressed as “G will actualise W1,” and W1 consequently becomes actual. G then knows the proposition “G actualised W1 rather than W0 because G intended W1.” The identifier is third-personal: it carries no first-personal mode of presentation. Yet the proposition identifies the source perfectly. It singles out the unique controller, connects that being’s intention to the resulting world, and excludes every alternative source. Nothing further supplied by “I actualised W1” would improve the attribution of the actualisation to G.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: The G-proposition identifies and causally explains the unique actualiser, but the argument requires representation of the outcome as the agent's own doing, not merely representation of G as a causal bearer. A rigid designator fixes reference without fixing the agent's stance toward the act. — *The response did not establish why the further agent-relative stance makes a responsibility-relevant difference. It concedes that this is the contested bridge premise rather than something secured by c008 alone.*
- **Defender B**: The relevant distinction should concern the cognition's role in intentional agency rather than its grammatical third-personal form. Third-personal source-identifying content alone does not guarantee a practically self-ascriptive representation of the actualisation. — *This revision avoids the objection to c008 as a claim about propositional form, but it requires an argument that practical self-ascription is necessary for robust responsibility and unavailable to the G-cognition.*

## Open questions
- What responsibility-relevant difference is made by representing an act under an agent-relative aspect once cognition already represents the agent's intention, control, and causal production of the outcome?
- Can a rigid third-personal identifier figure in a complete practical self-representation for the very agent it identifies?
- How should a practically self-ascriptive mode of presentation be individuated independently of the conclusion that irreducible de se content is required?
- Does robust contrastive responsibility require a stance of doing rather than merely accurate knowledge of one's intention-driven action?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the inference from third-personal source identification to the absence of responsibility-sufficient self-representation is invalid unless robust contrastive responsibility independently requires an agent-relative practical aspect. It would use the G case to show that third-personal cognition can represent intention, alternative control, causal production, and the unique source of actualisation, then anticipate the strongest reply: that these contents do not yet represent the act as the agent's own doing. The paper would need to specify that further stance without defining it as irreducible de se content, and show why its absence undermines robust responsibility even where the agent has complete third-personal knowledge and control.
