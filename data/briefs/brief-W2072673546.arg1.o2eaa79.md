## Challenged premise
**W2072673546.arg1.r1**: Schellenberg's analogical route for P1 is successful only if he independently shows that a vulnerable God is obligated to make the beloved's access to relationship available, rather than merely permitted to do so.

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · openai:gpt-5.6-terra*

Mira loves her estranged adult son, Leon, who has repeatedly said he wants reconciliation but is ashamed to initiate contact. Mira knows Leon’s current address and sends a plain letter: “I want a relationship with you; call me anytime.” She does not independently prove a general obligation that vulnerable parents must make reconciliation accessible; such a proof would require settling every possible family circumstance. Yet in this case, the letter successfully shows that Mira’s vulnerability is compatible with, and helps explain, her making access available: she risks rejection precisely because love makes Leon’s response matter. The analogy can therefore support the relevant claim about vulnerable divine love without first establishing an independent obligation to provide access.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: The original Mira case establishes only that vulnerability is compatible with, or can explain, offering access; compatibility and explanation do not establish an obligation. P1 requires the stronger claim that perfect love must make access available. — *The objector strengthened the case with Leon’s expressed desire, inability to initiate, low-cost communication, and absence of countervailing concerns. Defender A conceded that these features support a local obligation, so the requirement of a prior general obligation was too strong.*
- **Defender A**: Mira’s local obligation transfers to God only if the features grounding it also hold in the divine case; differences concerning dignity, safety, divine means of ensuring encounter, and temporary versus permanent nonbelief require further argument. — *This reply preserves a revised, conditional challenge rather than the original premise. It identifies a genuine bridge problem but does not establish that the relevant features are absent from all cases of nonresistant nonbelief.*
- **Defender B**: The analogy may make divine openness motivationally intelligible, but it cannot establish P1’s universal expectation without grounds in the divine case analogous to Leon’s desire, inability to initiate, and Mira’s low-cost opportunity to respond. — *It succeeds as a narrower revision of r1, but concedes that an analogy need not first derive a general obligation. Its remaining force depends on specifying and defending the required case-sensitive bridge grounds.*
