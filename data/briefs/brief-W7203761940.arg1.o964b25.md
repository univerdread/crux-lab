## Challenged premise
**W7203761940.c011**: Historical evidence alone lacks the directional signature expected if history were steered toward the good.

## Objection
*tradition_lens · anthropic:opus*

Consider Mara, a nurse whose village is destroyed in a war she did nothing to provoke, while her corrupt mayor escapes unharmed. Outwardly, the suffering is morally indiscriminate. But God knows what every free creature would freely do in every possible circumstance, and he actualized this world for that reason. Among the worlds he could create, only those containing this war place Mara in a border camp where she freely founds a clinic. In that camp a young officer freely protects the clinic at cost to himself. That act leads him to repent of atrocities he would otherwise have committed for decades. Here God steers history by arranging the circumstances of free choices, not by making rewards follow character. Each step looks like ordinary contingency, and the good aimed at is spread across persons, generations and eternity. So a history steered toward the good among free creatures should carry no visible trend at all. It should look just like what historians actually find.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: The Mara case does not exclude divine steering; it shows that a simple expectation of visible moral governance is not the only theistic account. Worldly evidence may therefore underdetermine the choice between naturalism and theism. — *A accepted that this response requires weakening c011. He could not identify an aggregate measure that Molinism predicts, so the original expected-signature premise does no independent evidential work.*
- **Defender B**: A hidden network of goods mediated by free choices can accommodate the observed distribution of suffering, but impersonal causation and probability can accommodate it as well. Thus worldly randomness is evidentially ambiguous rather than evidence for providence. — *This preserves the conclusion that randomness supplies no clear winner, but it concedes the objection to c011 as originally formulated. Its replacement premise concerns the inability of history independently to establish providence, not an expected signature absent from history.*

## Open questions
- Can Molinism derive a non-arbitrary, empirically assessable aggregate prediction from middle knowledge and providential aims?
- Does a theistic commitment to repentance, character formation, and eschatological justice remove all expectation of visible desert-tracking outcomes, or only weaken it?
- When a theory accommodates any observed history through hidden counterfactual goods, does that make the evidence neutral, or does it reduce the theory's explanatory or evidential value?
- Can the argument for c041 be established without c011, and what precise neutrality premise would be sufficient?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that a Molinist model of providence undermines the claim that benevolent historical steering should exhibit a visible directional signature, while leaving open whether the resulting accommodation of apparently random suffering is evidential neutrality or explanatory immunization. It would develop the structural point that providence through free choices, temporally dispersed goods, and deferred justice need not optimize earthly desert, then anticipate the strongest reply: that this only shows ambiguity and so supports the argument's final conclusion. To succeed, the paper would need to distinguish carefully between revising c011 into a neutrality premise and defending its original evidential role, and determine whether Molinism supplies any principled observable prediction at all.
