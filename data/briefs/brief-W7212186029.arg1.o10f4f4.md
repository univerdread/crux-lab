## Challenged premise
**W7212186029.c013**: Instantiation can be modelled as parthood because objects may be understood as mereological fusions of their properties.

## Objection
*tradition_lens · anthropic:opus*

Consider a chemist, Mara, studying a copper wire. She believes physical objects are composed of their physical constituents: atoms, fields, and their arrangements. She heats the wire, and it loses its coiled shape while every atom stays put. If the wire were a fusion of its properties, losing the property 'coiled' would mean losing a part. But nothing material left the wire. The wire also gains the property 'being thought of by Mara' when she reflects on it, yet no part arrives. The wire's real composition, the things that would show up in any physical inventory, is constant through both changes. Its properties track how its parts are arranged and related. They are not further ingredients. Treating instantiation as parthood confuses what an object is made of with what is true of it. The God case depends on this confusion: omniscience can be a 'part' of God only if parthood has been redefined until it no longer tracks composition. At that point, 'having no proper parts' says nothing about what God is made of.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: The wire case shows only that properties are not physical constituents. The proposed relation is explicitly similarity-relative, so it is a formal model of structural relations between God and attributes rather than an inventory of material parts. — *This reply concedes that the model does not by itself state the traditional denial of metaphysical composition. It therefore leaves an equivocation between the constituent sense of "parts" in the initial premise and the similarity-relative sense in the conclusion.*
- **Defender A**: God need not have contingent proper parts when relational properties are acquired, because the model counts only relevant similarity-parts; it does not guarantee that God is similar to every relational property in the required way. — *The response blocks an automatic inference from every relational property to a divine part, but it does not specify the relevance or similarity conditions. Without such conditions, it remains unclear whether the account is non-trivial and whether it excludes contingent relational properties principledly.*
- **Defender B**: The property-fusion account should be restricted to divine attributes and treated as non-compositional. Mutual parthood then explains how God and an attribute can be distinct without either standing in non-symmetric proper parthood to the other. — *Restricting the model avoids the most direct lessons of the wire case, but it does not establish that non-compositional similarity-based parthood captures metaphysical constituency. The restriction thus requires the bridging premise Defender A identifies.*

## Open questions
- What criteria determine which divine attributes or relational properties count as relevant similarity-parts?
- Can a relation that is explicitly non-compositional nevertheless track metaphysical constituency closely enough for the traditional doctrine?
- Does the formal profile assigned to similarity-relative proper parthood prevent trivial mutual parthood between God and attributes?
- What independent reason supports applying the property-fusion model to divine attributes but not to ordinary objects or arbitrary relational properties?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the symmetric-parthood formulation of divine simplicity succeeds only if its similarity-relative parthood relation is shown to represent the constituent relation excluded by traditional simplicity. It would distinguish the formal reconstruction from ordinary material composition, assess the proposed restriction to divine attributes, and anticipate the reply that formal modelling need not reproduce a physical inventory. To establish the thesis, it would need independently motivated criteria for relevant similarity-parts and an account of why those criteria make the relation sufficiently constituency-tracking, non-trivial, and resistant to contingent relational-property cases.
