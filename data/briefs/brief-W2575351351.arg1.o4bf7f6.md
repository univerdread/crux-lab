## Challenged premise
**W2575351351.c014**: Multiple goods may arise from a single instance of nonresistant nonbelief without increasing that evil.

## Objection
*blind_thought_experimenter · anthropic:opus*

Mara is a sincere seeker who wants to believe in God but cannot. Consider three goods her nonbelief might produce. First, she shows moral courage by sheltering a refugee without any expectation of divine reward. That needs her nonbelief to last through the crisis, about two months. Second, she comes to deeply understand other doubters. That needs her to feel the full desolation of unanswered searching, which takes a sharper and lonelier phase of doubt, not just mild uncertainty. Third, her brother freely chooses to walk with her through doubt rather than being prompted by evident divine presence. He is slow to choose, so her nonbelief must persist until he does, eighteen months in all. Each good attaches to the same episode of nonbelief, but each requires that episode to be longer or more painful than the previous goods required. Securing all three turns two calm months of doubt into a year and a half of anguish. The goods are not extra fruit from a fixed cost. Each one buys itself by enlarging the evil.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: The defender concedes that goods requiring an episode to be longer or more painful are not benefits gained at a fixed cost. The relevant question is instead whether the combined value of those goods exceeds the increased loss of relationship with God caused by the extended episode. — *This reply preserves only a restricted version of the premise and leaves the required comparative weighing undone. It does not show that the additional goods actually outweigh the additional relationship-loss.*
- **Defender B**: The defender argues that the premise is existential: several goods may arise within an independently bounded episode without requiring any further extension or intensification. The appropriate comparison is between the total loss of the maximally demanding warranted episode and the goods warranted by, or cost-free within, that episode. — *The reply establishes only the possibility of genuine overlap. It concedes that multiplicity alone does not establish that the resulting goods outweigh the total loss of relationship, which is the work needed for the original inference.*

## Open questions
- What could make the duration and severity of an episode of nonresistant nonbelief independently fixed, rather than determined by the goods invoked to justify it?
- How should the lost value of relationship with God be compared with goods that both warrant and arise within a temporary episode of nonbelief?
- Can defenders identify plausible cases in which several substantial goods are genuinely co-realized without adding duration or intensity to the episode?
- Does restricting the relevant aggregate to the warranting good plus cost-free co-realized goods leave enough value for the many-goods response to succeed?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the many-goods response requires a marginal-cost constraint: goods associated with nonresistant nonbelief may be aggregated only after distinguishing goods that increase the episode's duration or severity from goods genuinely realized within an already warranted episode. The case would use escalating examples such as Mara's to show why an unqualified appeal to several goods mismeasures the evil, anticipate the reply that some bounded episodes contain cost-free overlap, and show either that such overlap cannot plausibly bear the required justificatory weight or specify conditions under which the restricted response can meet the total relationship-loss.
