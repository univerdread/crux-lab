## Challenged premise
**W2575351351.c014**: Multiple goods may arise from a single instance of nonresistant nonbelief without increasing that evil.

## Objection
*tradition_lens · anthropic:opus*

Belief in God is properly basic. It arises from a faculty that, working as designed, produces it in ordinary circumstances. Nonresistant nonbelief therefore marks that faculty as dulled, and the dulling is itself the evil. Take Maria, raised secular and sincerely open, whose sense of the divine stays silent. Defenders cite three goods from her single case. The first is a freely undertaken search for God, which needs her faculty dulled only until she begins seeking. The second is solidarity with fellow doubters, which needs it dulled through years of shared uncertainty. The third is moral courage without assurance of divine backing, which needs it fully inert at the moment of her ethical crisis, long after her search began. These goods do not share one fixed evil. Each further good requires the malfunction to run longer, reach deeper, or recur at a new point. A truth-aimed faculty failing for longer and more thoroughly is a larger evil. So stacking goods on her nonbelief enlarges the very evil they are meant to offset.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: The goods may arise from one shared interval of nonbelief rather than from separate or progressively deeper malfunctions. Search, solidarity, and moral courage can all be consequences of the same period in which Maria doubts. — *This response did not establish that the listed goods share one bounded interval. It conceded that the original premise needs an explicit restriction to such an episode and a measure of its evil.*
- **Defender B**: Several goods can be consequences of a temporally fixed profile of nonbelief, so counting them separately need not itself lengthen, deepen, or repeat the silence. The relevant comparison should be between that profile and its alternatives. — *The objector argued that God determines the profile rather than inherits it, so the comparison must include the shorter period sufficient for the first good. Defender B conceded that life-spread goods cannot be treated as costless merely because they occur within one biographical episode.*

## Open questions
- What individuates a single episode of nonresistant nonbelief: a continuous period, a fixed psychological profile, or a loss of relationship?
- Should the evil of nonbelief be measured by malfunction in a properly basic belief-forming faculty, by the duration of a lost relationship with God, or by both?
- When goods occur at different times, what makes the longer period of nonbelief necessary for their joint realization rather than merely permitted by it?
- What standard of comparability should govern alternatives: must they realize the same goods, or an adequately comparable set of goods with no greater evil?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the many-goods response requires a comparative, episode-sensitive account of nonresistant nonbelief: several goods may support a single period of hiddenness only when their joint realization requires no additional nonbelief-related evil relative to available alternatives that secure an adequately comparable set of goods. The case would distinguish genuinely overlapping goods from goods distributed across a life and show why merely redescribing an extended deprivation as one instance does not settle its cost. It would anticipate the strongest reply that all goods can be consequences of one fixed interval, and would need to show either that the interval is necessary for the combined goods or that relevant alternatives impose equal or greater evil.
