## Challenged premise
**W2575351351.c014**: Multiple goods may arise from a single instance of nonresistant nonbelief without increasing that evil.

## Objection
*tradition_lens · anthropic:opus*

Belief in God is properly basic. It arises from a faculty that, working as designed, produces it in ordinary circumstances. Nonresistant nonbelief therefore marks that faculty as dulled, and the dulling is itself the evil. Take Maria, raised secular and sincerely open, whose sense of the divine stays silent. Defenders cite three goods from her single case. The first is a freely undertaken search for God, which needs her faculty dulled only until she begins seeking. The second is solidarity with fellow doubters, which needs it dulled through years of shared uncertainty. The third is moral courage without assurance of divine backing, which needs it fully inert at the moment of her ethical crisis, long after her search began. These goods do not share one fixed evil. Each further good requires the malfunction to run longer, reach deeper, or recur at a new point. A truth-aimed faculty failing for longer and more thoroughly is a larger evil. So stacking goods on her nonbelief enlarges the very evil they are meant to offset.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: The goods need not require distinct or progressively deeper failures of a belief-forming faculty: search, solidarity, and courage may arise within one shared interval of doubt. The number of goods supported by an interval does not itself increase the evil of that interval. — *The defender could not establish from Maria's case that the goods genuinely share a bounded interval rather than require nonbelief at distinct times. The reply also initially relocated the evil from faculty malfunction to lost relationship without defending that move.*
- **Defender B**: A temporally fixed episode of nonbelief can have several consequences without becoming longer, deeper, or recurrent merely because those consequences are counted separately. The many-goods defense should compare that fixed episode with relevant alternatives. — *The objector showed that the relevant comparison is not simply within a stipulated episode, but with the shorter period needed to secure earlier goods. Defender B conceded that life-spread goods may involve added deprivation unless their joint realization requires the longer profile relative to adequately comparable alternatives.*

## Open questions
- What individuates an instance of nonresistant nonbelief: a faculty malfunction, a lost relationship, a temporally bounded episode, or some combination of these?
- Does the claim that belief in God is properly basic entail that prolonged nonbelief is a progressively greater faculty-related evil?
- When several goods occur across a life, what makes a longer period of nonbelief necessary for their joint realization rather than merely permitted by it?
- What standard determines whether an alternative realizes an adequately comparable set of goods for purposes of the relevant counterfactual comparison?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that the many-goods response requires a comparative account of the nonbelief-related evil involved in realizing several goods, rather than the bare claim that multiple goods can arise from one instance of nonresistant nonbelief. It would distinguish genuinely overlapping goods, which may depend on one necessary bounded interval, from life-spread goods, whose joint realization may require added duration or severity relative to shorter alternatives. The strongest reply to anticipate is that a single fixed episode can support many consequences without multiplication of evil; the paper would need to show why fixing the episode does not settle the relevant divine choice, and specify a defensible measure of the evil and a standard for comparing alternatives.
