## Challenged premise
**W7203761940.c011**: Historical evidence alone lacks the directional signature expected if history were steered toward the good.

## Objection
*tradition_lens · anthropic:opus*

Consider Mara, a nurse whose village is destroyed in a war she did nothing to provoke, while her corrupt mayor escapes unharmed. Outwardly, the suffering is morally indiscriminate. But God knows what every free creature would freely do in every possible circumstance, and he actualized this world for that reason. Among the worlds he could create, only those containing this war place Mara in a border camp where she freely founds a clinic. In that camp a young officer freely protects the clinic at cost to himself. That act leads him to repent of atrocities he would otherwise have committed for decades. Here God steers history by arranging the circumstances of free choices, not by making rewards follow character. Each step looks like ordinary contingency, and the good aimed at is spread across persons, generations and eternity. So a history steered toward the good among free creatures should carry no visible trend at all. It should look just like what historians actually find.

## Strongest responses and why they failed
- **Defender A**: The objection shows only that providence is not excluded by ordinary contingency; it supports the broader conclusion that worldly randomness underdetermines the choice between naturalism and theism. — *This preserves the conclusion only by abandoning c011's claim that benevolent steering was expected to leave a directional signature. Defender A ultimately conceded that no measurable aggregate trend is predicted on the Molinist account.*
- **Defender B**: A hidden network of counterfactual goods does not make randomness evidence for divine steering, because impersonal causation and hidden providence can both accommodate the same observed distribution. — *The reply successfully supports evidential ambiguity, but it revises c011 from a premise about an expected but absent signature into a premise about the inability of historical evidence to establish providence independently.*

## Open questions
- Does Molinism imply any measurable aggregate historical pattern beyond desert-tracking outcomes?
- What constraints, if any, prevent appeals to remote counterfactual goods and deferred justice from accommodating every possible history?
- Can evidential neutrality between naturalism and providence be established without assuming that both accounts have equal explanatory flexibility?
- Does the argument's conclusion require c011 at all once biblical theology denies that earthly outcomes track moral character?

## Paper direction
A paper here would argue that Molinist providence undermines the claim that benevolent historical steering should produce a visible aggregate moral signature, while leaving open whether the resulting neutrality is principled or merely immunizing. The case would distinguish the failure of desert-tracking outcomes from the absence of all possible providential evidence, anticipate the reply that hidden providence and impersonal causation are simply evidentially ambiguous, and show either that Molinism supplies a non-arbitrary constraint on acceptable historical distributions or that c011 should be removed from the argument because it does no independent evidential work.
